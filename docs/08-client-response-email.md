**Subject:** The new CGS website — built to help with the numbers, not just to look good

Dear Jayant,

Thank you for being so open about where the committee stands. You are weighing a
fair question: with student and teacher numbers falling, is a website really
worth it? That is exactly the question I set out to answer. So rather than just
make the old site look nicer, I have rebuilt it to do one job — help bring in
**new students and new teachers**.

You can see all of it live here, and I would genuinely love you to click around
before you decide:

**https://gujaratischools.vercel.app**

Here is what it now does for the two numbers that matter:

1. **Families can find a class.** "Find a school" lists real Gujarati schools
   across the UK, searchable by town. A parent finds their nearest class in
   seconds and sends an enquiry with one tap — and it comes straight to CGS.

2. **New teachers can step forward.** "Become a teacher" says plainly what is
   true: you do not need to be qualified, we train you. A willing volunteer can
   register their interest in under a minute.

3. **Joining is effortless.** People can apply for membership online in a short
   form, or download the PDF as before. Online applications reach you straight
   away, and the site tells the applicant they will hear back within 12 hours —
   a small promise that turns interest into members. (We can change that
   timeframe whenever you like.)

4. **It works around the clock, for free.** A parent searching at ten at night,
   a volunteer reading on their phone — the website quietly does the outreach
   for CGS every single day, at no ongoing cost.

Two things the committee had asked for are also done:

- **Teachers can actually find the resources now** — every past paper, training
  pack and form, searchable, in a couple of taps, on a phone.
- **You can keep it up to date yourselves** — adding a school, a news post or a
  document is simple, with no developer needed. Each member school can even keep
  its own listing current.

Nothing on the site has been invented — every school, document and detail comes
from CGS's own records.

My honest view: the website is now the cheapest and hardest-working way CGS has
to reach new families and new teachers. There is no risk in going ahead — it is
already built and live for you to try today.

## Add this to the email — being found online, and credibility

**A note on being found online**

The behind-the-scenes work that helps Google understand and list the site is now
done — clear page titles and descriptions, a sitemap, the right structured data,
and a fast, mobile-first build. That part is sorted.

But being found is only half the battle. When a parent searches "Gujarati
classes near me," or someone wants to help teach, they often land on somebody
else's page — because the old site wasn't built around the words people actually
search for, and it looked dated. So competitors quietly pick up the very
students and volunteers CGS is trying to reach.

Here's the reality today: no teacher finds a role, and no family finds a class,
by asking around in person any more. They search online, and they decide in a
few seconds — by how trustworthy the page looks and how easily they can act.
That first impression is where credibility is won or lost now. This rebuild is
made to win it: the right words in the right places, and a site that looks and
feels like an organisation worth joining.
