# The website as a growth engine — note for the CGS committee

*Context: the committee is weighing whether the website is worth it while
student and teacher numbers are declining. This note reframes the site around
exactly that concern.*

## The honest starting point

Numbers are falling. A prettier website does not, on its own, reverse that. So
the question isn't "do we want a nicer site" — it's "can the website do real
work against the decline?" The rebuild is now pointed squarely at that.

## What actually drives the two numbers

**More students** come from one thing above all: a family being able to find a
Gujarati class near them, easily, at the moment they're looking. Today that
information is buried. On the old site a parent had to already know the page
existed and scroll a wall of text.

**More teachers** come from removing the fear that you must be a qualified
teacher to help. Most CGS teachers never were — they're parents and volunteers
who were trained. That message was nowhere near the front door.

So the rebuilt home page now leads, directly under the headline, with two doors:

1. **Learn Gujarati → Find a school near you.** A real, searchable directory of
   **32 schools across 23+ towns**, grouped by region, with addresses. A parent
   in Luton or Wembley finds their class in seconds. This is the student-growth
   engine, running 24/7, for free.

2. **Teach Gujarati → You don't need to be qualified, we train you.** A warm
   recruitment page that lowers the barrier and routes a willing volunteer
   straight into training and membership. This is the teacher-growth engine.

## Why this is the cheapest growth CGS has

Every other way of growing numbers costs money or volunteer hours — events,
leaflets, word of mouth. The website does it while everyone sleeps: a parent
searching "Gujarati class near me" at 10pm finds a member school; a volunteer
reading "we'll train you" signs up. It is the lowest-cost, highest-leverage
channel the charity owns, and it is now built to do that job.

## It also fixes the two original complaints

- **"Teachers can't find resources."** The whole library — 378 documents,
  past papers back to 2012, every training pack — is searchable and filterable,
  reachable in two clicks from any page, on a phone.
- **"Nobody updates the site."** Every news post, resource and now every school
  listing is a simple file the committee edits — no developer, no CMS. The
  schools directory gives each member school its own reason to keep the site
  current, which keeps it alive.

## What would make it even stronger — and needs the committee

The schools directory is seeded from CGS's own published list. To make it a
genuine recruitment asset, the committee can, over time:

- **Confirm and complete each school's details** — day and time of classes, a
  contact name, whether they're taking new children. A directory that says
  "Tuesdays 6.25pm, taking new pupils" converts far better than a name alone.
- **Add the schools missing from the old list** — the network is ~30
  organisations; the site lists what was public. Filling the rest makes the map
  look as alive as the charity actually is.
- **Add a short "why learn Gujarati" line from a parent or two** — real voices
  move families more than any statistic.

None of this blocks launch. The site works today. These are the levers that turn
it from "a good website" into "the reason our numbers stopped falling."

## The recommendation

Go ahead. Not because the site is handsome — because it is now the one asset
that markets CGS to new families and new teachers continuously, at no ongoing
cost, and because it makes the network's real reach visible at a moment when the
committee itself needs to see it.
